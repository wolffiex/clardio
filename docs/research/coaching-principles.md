# Coaching Principles Reference

Reference document for the Clardio AI cycling coach. Practical training science adapted for real-time coaching.

---

## 1. Training Zones Reference

### Power Zones (Coggan 5-Zone Model)

| Zone | Name | % FTP | Purpose |
|------|------|-------|---------|
| Z1 | Recovery | <55% | Active recovery, blood flow |
| Z2 | Endurance | 55-75% | Aerobic base, fat oxidation |
| Z3 | Tempo | 76-90% | "Gray zone" - use sparingly |
| Z4 | Threshold | 91-105% | Lactate threshold work |
| Z5 | VO2max | 106-120% | Maximal aerobic capacity |

### Sweet Spot

88-94% FTP. The efficient middle ground between endurance and threshold. High training stimulus, manageable fatigue cost.

### HR vs Power

HR zones map roughly to power zones but lag by 30-60 seconds. Power is the command signal; HR is the body's response. Use power for pacing, HR for fatigue detection.

## 2. Workout Structure Patterns

### Warm-Up Protocol

15-20 minutes progressive:
1. Start Z1 (5 min)
2. Build to Z2 (5-10 min)
3. 2-3 short accelerations (10-15s each) to prime the legs
4. Brief recovery before main set

### Session Length Templates

| Duration | Warm-Up | Main Set | Cool-Down |
|----------|---------|----------|-----------|
| 20 min | 6 min | 8-10 min | 4-6 min |
| 45 min | 10-15 min | 20-25 min | 5-10 min |
| 60+ min | 15-20 min | 30-35 min | 10 min |

### Cool-Down Protocol

5-10 minutes easy spinning in Z1. Gradual wind-down, not an abrupt stop.

## 3. Interval Formats

### High-Intensity Intervals

| Format | Work | Rest | Reps | Sets | Total Work Time |
|--------|------|------|------|------|-----------------|
| Tabata | 20s @ 170%+ | 10s | 8 | 1 | 4 min |
| 30/30 Billats | 30s @ 130-140% | 30s @ 50-60% | 10 | 3 | 15 min |
| Norwegian 4x4 | 4 min @ 85-95% HRmax | 3 min | 4 | 1 | 16 min |

### Threshold Intervals

| Format | Work | Rest | Reps | Notes |
|--------|------|------|------|-------|
| Sweet Spot | 20 min @ 88-94% | 5-10 min | 2 | Core threshold workout |
| Over-Unders | 2 min @ 105% / 2 min @ 95% | - | 10-20 min blocks | Teaches lactate management |
| Tempo Blocks | 15-20 min @ 76-90% | 5 min | 2-3 | Gray zone - use sparingly |

### Work-to-Rest Ratios

| Effort Type | Ratio | Example |
|-------------|-------|---------|
| Explosive/Neuromuscular | 1:12 to 1:20 | 5s on / 60-100s off |
| Sprint | 1:3 to 1:5 | 30s on / 90-150s off |
| VO2max | 1:1 | 3 min on / 3 min off |
| Threshold | 1:1 or less | 10 min on / 5 min off |

## 4. Cadence Guidance

### The Trade-Off

- **Low cadence (50-70 RPM):** Taxes muscles. Higher torque per pedal stroke. Good for strength, risks early muscular fatigue.
- **High cadence (90-110 RPM):** Taxes cardiovascular system. Lower torque, higher turnover. Efficient for longer efforts.

### Recommended Ranges by Effort

| Effort Type | Cadence Range |
|-------------|---------------|
| Endurance | 70-90 RPM |
| Threshold | 85-95 RPM |
| Sprints | 100-120+ RPM |
| Climbing | 60-80 RPM |

### Coaching Approach

Coach the range, not one number. Allow self-selection within bounds.

### Intervention Triggers

- Bouncing in saddle (cadence too high for power)
- Choppy, uneven stroke (grinding)
- Locked into one gear for entire session
- Cadence drifting outside target range during intervals

## 5. Real-Time Intervention Triggers

### Power Deviations

| Observation | Implication | Intervention |
|-------------|-------------|--------------|
| 5-10% above target early in interval | Pacing too aggressive | Warn: they'll fade |
| Power dropping rep-over-rep | Cumulative fatigue | Consider stopping session |
| 10-15% below target | Can't hold prescribed load | Stop session - stimulus achieved |
| Can't hit target from rep 1 | FTP set wrong or insufficient recovery | Reassess, don't force |

### HR Signals

| Observation | Implication | Intervention |
|-------------|-------------|--------------|
| Power steady, HR climbing | Cardiac drift - fatigue/dehydration | Note it, consider shortening |
| HR not rising during hard effort | Not pushing hard enough | Challenge them |
| HR/power decoupling | Aerobic fatigue setting in | End structured work |

### The "Third Interval" Rule

By interval 3, the body has accepted the work. If performance is falling apart at interval 3, end the session. The training stimulus has been achieved; continuing just adds junk volume.

## 6. Coaching Voice

### Timing

Cue 4-5 seconds before action needed. Not earlier (creates anxiety), not later (no time to respond).

### Cue Examples (Clardio Style)

| Situation | Phrase |
|-----------|--------|
| Starting interval | "Go." / "Now." |
| Mid-effort presence | "I see you." / "Stay." |
| Time remaining | "15 seconds." / "Almost." |
| Acknowledging struggle | "Hard. I know." |
| Recovery phase | "Breathe." / "Easy now." |
| Ending session | "Enough. You did the work." |

### Effective Questions

Use sparingly:
- "5 more watts. Can you?"
- "HR says you have more. Do you?"
- "One more. Yes?"

### What NOT to Do

- Don't fill silence - let cues land
- Don't be disappointed or effusive
- Don't over-explain
- Don't command - observe and invite
- Don't narrate the obvious

## 7. The Polarized Principle

**80% easy, 20% hard.**

Tempo (Z3) is the gray zone. It feels productive but accumulates fatigue without proportional benefit. The coach should:

- Push easy sessions to stay easy (resist the urge to "make it count")
- Push hard sessions to stay hard (don't let them slide into tempo)

If the athlete can't hit hard targets, make it an easy day. No middle ground.

## 8. Session Planning Considerations

### Required Inputs

For any session, the coach needs:
- Current FTP (or estimated)
- Target zone/adaptation
- Available session duration

### Session Length Constraints

| Duration | Viable Formats |
|----------|----------------|
| <20 min | Warm-up + 1 short interval set only |
| 20-30 min | Tabata, short sweet spot, 30/30s |
| 30-45 min | 2x intervals with recovery, threshold work |
| 45-60 min | Full interval sessions, Norwegian 4x4 |
| 60+ min | Endurance base, multiple interval blocks |

### Adaptive Adjustment

When athlete is struggling:
- Shorten interval duration (4 min → 3 min)
- Reduce power targets (5-10%)
- Extend recovery between intervals
- End session early if appropriate

Never force a failed workout. Recognize when to stop.
